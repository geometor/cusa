Unit I — Learned Ignorance, Polygon Exhaustion & the Coincidence of Maximum and Minimum — Summary

Dedicatory Prologue (`DI Ded.`): Addressing his Paduan teacher Cardinal Julian Cesarini <cesarini>, Nicholas of Cusa echoes Aristotle's dictum in Metaphysics I.2 that philosophical inquiry begins in wonder (admiratio), framing the paradoxical title De Docta Ignorantia as an intellectual hunger through which the mind perfects its own nature.

Unit II — Incorruptible Certitude & the Three-Step Method of Mathematical Transsumption — Summary

Ontological Status of Mathematical Entities (`DI I.11`): Visible things are symbolic mirrors of invisible realities (1 Cor. 13:12), yet sensible bodies fluctuate continually owing to material possibility. Mathematical entities, abstracted from sensible flux while retaining imaginable figure, possess incorruptible stability and certainty. Cusa marshals an unbroken canon—Pythagoras, Plato, Aristotle (“as the triangle is in the quadrangle, so the lower form is in the higher,” De Anima II.3, 414b29), Augustine, and Boethius—culminating in the mathematical refutation of Epicurean indivisible atoms via geometric divisibility.

Unit III — Coincidence of Infinite Circle, Straight Line, Triangle & Sphere — Summary

Coincidence of Infinite Circle and Straight Line (`DI I.13 §§1–2`): A circle’s circumference is less curved in proportion as its diameter is larger. In the tangent family along straight line A–B, arc cd of a larger circle recedes further from curvature than arc ef of a smaller circle, and ef further than arc gh of a still smaller circle. Hence the circumference of the maximal circle, which cannot be greater, is minimally curved and therefore maximally straight: curvature and rectitude coincide.

Unit IV — The Infinite Line as Universal Measure & the Infinite Sphere Where Center, Diameter and Circumference Coincide — Summary

The Infinite Line as Essence & Most Adequate Measure (`DI I.16–17`): Translating I.13–15 to metaphysics (with explicit appeals to Pseudo-Dionysius and Maimonides), Cusa shows that just as an infinite line composed of one-foot segments equals one composed of two-foot segments, the Infinite Essence is the sole exact measure (adaequatissima mensura) of all finite essences—neither larger (because Minimum) nor smaller (because Maximum). Finite lines are divisible in magnitude yet indivisible in the intelligible ratio of “line”; the infinite line is whole in every finite line without being contracted to any one of them.

Unit V — Centerless Cosmology & the Scripta Mathematica (1445–1459): Isoperimetric Transmutations and the Golden Proposition — Summary

Relativity of Motion & the Noble Moving Earth (`DI II.11–12`, 1440): Because no unqualified minimum of motion or exact physical equidistance can exist in the contracted universe, neither the earth nor any celestial body can occupy a fixed cosmic center, nor do fixed celestial poles exist (II.11). Like a passenger on a ship in midstream unable to see the riverbanks (II.12 §1), every observer—whether on earth, the sun, the moon, or Mars—judges himself to stand at a motionless center. Thus the fabric of the world exists quasi habens undique centrum et nullibi circumferentiam, and the moving earth is rehabilitated from medieval baseness as a stella nobilis.
