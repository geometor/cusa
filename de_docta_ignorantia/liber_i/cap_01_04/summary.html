Learned Ignorance, Polygon Exhaustion & the Coincidence of Maximum and Minimum — Structural Synopsis¶

Argumentative Architecture & Interactive Diagram Guide · DI Ded., I.1–4, Ep. (1440)

I. Argumentative Synopsis¶

  1. Dedicatory Prologue (`DI Ded.`): Addressing his Paduan teacher Cardinal Julian Cesarini <cesarini>, Nicholas of Cusa echoes Aristotle's dictum in Metaphysics I.2 that philosophical inquiry begins in wonder (admiratio), framing the paradoxical title De Docta Ignorantia as an intellectual hunger through which the mind perfects its own nature.

  2. Comparative Proportion & the Inaccessibility of the Infinite (`DI I.1`): Every discursive investigation judges the unknown through comparative proportion to something already presupposed as certain, just as later geometric theorems reduce through chains of media to first axioms. Because proportion requires agreement and difference articulated by number, and the infinite escapes every finite ratio (infiniti ad finitum proportio non est), the infinite qua infinite remains unknowable to comparative reason. Like Socrates and Aristotle's night-owl before the sun, human wisdom culminates in knowing its own ignorance.

  3. Threefold Architecture of the Maximum (`DI I.2`): Cusa defines the Maximum in Anselmian terms as quo nihil maius esse potest and outlines the three books of the treatise: Book I investigates the Absolute Maximum (God); Book II investigates the contracted universal maximum (the cosmos existing solely in plurality through contraction); Book III investigates the maximum at once contracted and absolute.

  4. Polygon Exhaustion & Ungraspable Precision (`DI I.3`): Among finite things equality is always gradual; between measure and measured an irreducible gap remains. Hence the finite intellect stands to truth as an inscribed polygon stands to a circle: multiplying the angles increases likeness indefinitely without ever producing identity unless the polygon resolves itself into the circle.

  5. Coincidence of Maximum and Minimum (`DI I.4`) & Sea-Voyage Colophon (`DI Ep.`): Because the Absolute Maximum admits neither exceeding nor exceeded, nothing can oppose it; stripped of quantitative contraction into “large” and “small,” superlative Maximum and superlative Minimum coincide (coincidentia oppositorum). The concluding letter records Cusa’s illumination at sea returning from Constantinople (winter 1437/1438) and dates completion at Kues to February 12, 1440.
