Liber I — De Maximo Absoluto¶

De Docta Ignorantia · Liber Primus — Nicholas of Cusa (1440)

Nicholas of Cusa (Nicolaus Cusanus, 1401–1464)

Liber Primus: De Maximo Absoluto
Dedicated to Cardinal Julian Cesarini (Dedicatio ad Dominum Iulianum Cardinalem)
Completed at Kues on February 12, 1440 (Epistola Auctoris)

Book I investigates the Absolute Maximum—that than which nothing can be greater—which transcends all comparative proportion and in which the superlative Minimum coincides. Through the mathematical transsumption of the straight line, triangle, circle, and sphere, Cusa demonstrates how geometric symbols serve as an incorruptible ladder to intellectual vision.

Capitula of Book I¶

Dedicatory Prologue to Cardinal Julian Cesarini

Ita recte puto admirari, propter quod philosophari, sciendi desiderium praevenire, ut intellectus, cuius intelligere est esse, studio veritatis perficiatur.

In just this way I judge that wonder, on account of which philosophy begins, runs ahead of the desire to know, so that the intellect, whose very being is to understand, may be brought to perfection through the study of truth. — De Docta Ignorantia, Dedicatio §2

How To Know Is Not-To-Know: Comparative Proportion & Socratic Ignorance

Omnis igitur inquisitio in comparativa proportione facili vel difficili existit; propter quod infinitum ut infinitum, cum omnem proportionem aufugiat, ignotum est.

Every inquiry, accordingly, consists in comparative proportion, whether easy or difficult; for which reason the infinite as infinite, since it escapes all proportion, remains unknown. — De Docta Ignorantia I.1 §2

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Preliminary Elucidation of the Absolute, Contracted, and Joined Maximum

Maximum autem hoc dico, quo nihil maius esse potest. Abundantia vero uni convenit. Coincidit itaque maximitati unitas, quae est et entitas.

Now I call ‘Maximum’ that than which nothing can be greater. Yet fullness belongs to the One. Unity, therefore—which is also beingness—coincides with maximality. — De Docta Ignorantia I.2 §1

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

That Precise Truth Is Incomprehensible: Polygon Exhaustion & the Circle

Intellectus igitur, qui non est veritas, numquam veritatem adeo praecise comprehendit, quin per infinitum praecisius comprehendi possit, habens se ad veritatem sicut polygonia ad circulum.

The intellect, therefore, which is not truth, never comprehends truth so precisely that it could not be comprehended infinitely more precisely, standing to truth as a polygon stands to a circle. — De Docta Ignorantia I.3 §2

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

The Absolute Maximum and the Coincidence of Maximum and Minimum

Maximum itaque absolutum cum sit omne id quod esse potest, est penitus in actu; et sicut non potest esse maius, eadem ratione nec minus, cum sit omne id quod esse potest. Minimum autem est, quo minus esse non potest.

Because the Absolute Maximum is everything that can be, it is wholly in act; and just as it cannot be greater, for the very same reason neither can it be smaller, since it is all that can be. Now the Minimum is that than which nothing can be smaller. — De Docta Ignorantia I.4 §1

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Incorruptible Certitude of Mathematical Entities in Apprehending Divine Truths

In rebus enim incorruptibilibus et certis mathematicis signis utimur, quoniam corporalia in quadam instabili possibilitate fluctuant, mathematicalia vero abstracta stabilissima nobis sunt.

Therefore we employ mathematical signs, which are incorruptible and certain; for corporeal things fluctuate in a certain unstable possibility, whereas mathematical entities, being abstracted, possess the utmost stability for us. — De Docta Ignorantia I.11 §1

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

The Three-Step Method of Mathematical Transsumption

Primo necesse est figuras mathematicas finitas considerare cum suis passionibus et rationibus, et ipsas rationes correspondenter ad infinitas tales figuras transferre, post haec tertio adhuc altius ipsas rationes infinitarum figurarum transumere ad infinitum simplex absolutissimum etiam ab omni figura.

First we must consider finite mathematical figures with their properties and ratios, next transfer those ratios correspondingly to infinite figures of the same kind, and third transsume the ratios of those infinite figures still higher unto the Simple Infinite wholly absolved from every figure. — De Docta Ignorantia I.12 §1

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Properties of the Maximal Infinite Line: Curvature Resolves into Rectitude

Si esset linea infinita, illa esset recta, illa esset triangulus, illa esset circulus et esset sphaera; immo curvitas in ipsa maxima linea est rectitudo.

If there were an infinite line, it would be a straight line, a triangle, a circle, and a sphere; nay rather, within that maximal line itself curvature is rectitude. — De Docta Ignorantia I.13 §§1–2

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

That the Infinite Line Is a Triangle: Flattening Triangle & Triple Quad Limit

In omni enim triangulo duo latera simul iuncta tertio sunt maiora; sed si unum latus est infinitum, cetera non possunt esse minora.

In every triangle two sides joined together are greater than the third; yet if one side is infinite, the remaining sides cannot be smaller. — De Docta Ignorantia I.14 §1

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

That That Triangle Is a Circle and a Sphere: Conformal Inversion & Unfolding

Et quoniam linea infinita est in actu id omne, quod in potentia est finita: sequitur eam esse triangulum, circulum et sphaeram.

And since the infinite line is in act all that which the finite line is in potency, it follows that it is a triangle, a circle, and a sphere. — De Docta Ignorantia I.15 §3

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

The Infinite Line as Universal Essence & Most Adequate Measure

Ipsa igitur maxima linea est adaequatissima mensura omnium linearum, ita quod nec minor est, quia non exceditur ab aliqua, nec maior, quia nulla eam excedit.

Therefore the maximal line itself is the most adequate measure of all lines, being neither smaller—since it is not exceeded by any—nor larger, since none exceeds it. — De Docta Ignorantia I.16 §3

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Profoundest Doctrines Drawn from the Infinite Line: Indivisibility & Measure

Linea finita est divisibilis et infinita indivisibilis, quia infinitum non habet partes, in quo maximum coincidit cum minimo.

A finite line is divisible whereas the infinite line is indivisible, because the infinite—in which the Maximum coincides with the Minimum—has no parts. — De Docta Ignorantia I.17 §1

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Immediate and Mediate Participation in Rectitude and Beingness

Rectitudo igitur infinita est mensura rectitudinis et curvitatis; sed recta linea immediatius participat rectitudinem, curva vero mediate.

Infinite rectitude is therefore the measure both of straightness and of curvature; yet a straight line participates in rectitude immediately, whereas a curved line participates mediately. — De Docta Ignorantia I.18 §2

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Transsumption of the Infinite Triangle to the Maximal Trinity

Cum enim incipis numerare trinitatem, tunc exis veritatem. Unitas igitur est trinitas; et econverso trinitas unitas.

For when you begin to count the Trinity, you step outside the truth. Unity, therefore, is Trinity, and conversely Trinity is Unity. — De Docta Ignorantia I.19 §1

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Why a Quaternity or More Is Impossible: Minimal Polygon Theorem

Triangulus est minima figura polygonia, qua minor esse nequit; quadrangulus autem non est minima figura, quia componitur ex triangulis.

The triangle is the minimal polygonal figure, than which none can be smaller; whereas a quadrangle is not a minimal figure, because it is composed of triangles. — De Docta Ignorantia I.20 §2

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

The Infinite Circle Where Center, Diameter & Circumference Coincide

Patet ergo centrum, diametrum et circumferentiam idem esse. Ex quo docetur ignorantia nostra incomprehensibile maximum esse, cui minimum non opponitur; sed centrum est in ipso circumferentia.

Clearly, therefore, center, diameter, and circumference are identical; thereby our ignorance is taught that the incomprehensible Maximum is that to which the Minimum is not opposed, and that within it the center is the circumference. — De Docta Ignorantia I.21 §3

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

How Divine Providence Enfolds & Unites Contradictories

Posita complicatione non ponitur res complicata, sed posita explicatione ponitur complicatio.

When the enfolding is posited, the enfolded thing is not thereby posited, whereas when the unfolding is posited, the enfolding is necessarily posited. — De Docta Ignorantia I.22 §2

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Transsumption of the Infinite Sphere to the Actual Existence of God

In sphaera infinita longitudo, latitudo et profunditas concurrunt in centro; centrum enim est ipsa sphaera, et ipsius sphaerae actus est omnium motuum quies.

In the infinite sphere length, breadth, and depth concur in the center; for the center is the sphere itself, and the actuality of that sphere is the rest of all motions. — De Docta Ignorantia I.23 §1

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Author’s Epistolary Colophon: The Sea-Voyage Illumination (1440)

In mari me ex Graecia redeunte, credo superno dono a patre luminum, a quo omne datum optimum, ad hoc ductus sum, ut incomprehensibilia incomprehensibiliter amplecterer in docta ignorantia.

While I was returning by sea from Greece, I was led—I believe by a supernal gift from the Father of Lights, from whom comes every best gift—to embrace incomprehensible things incomprehensibly in learned ignorance. — De Docta Ignorantia, Epistola Auctoris §1
