On Learned Ignorance & Mathematical Writings (1440)¶

De Docta Ignorantia Libri Tres — Nicholas of Cusa (Kues, February 12, 1440)

Nicholas of Cusa (Nicolaus Cusanus, 1401–1464)
Cardinal Priest of San Pietro in Vincoli · Bishop of Brixen

First Edition: Paris (Jodocus Badius Ascensius, 1514, ed. Jacques Lefèvre d’Étaples)
Critical Editions: Bari (Paolo Rotta, 1913) · Heidelberg (Ernst Hoffmann & Raymond Klibansky, 1932)
GEOMETOR Digital Edition: 100% Original English Translation with Synchronized Latin Witness

De Docta Ignorantia presents in a critical Digital Scriptorium edition the complete geometric, epistemological, and cosmological core of Nicholas of Cusa's De Docta Ignorantia (completed at Kues on February 12, 1440).

Every chapter is presented across four synchronized tracks—English (a freshly authored, 100% original GEOMETOR translation rendered directly from the Latin), Latin (1440 / 1514–1913) (collated from Paolo Rotta's 1913 edition with Raymond Klibansky's archival restorations and the 1514 Paris / 1565 Basel Opera), Summary, and Critical Notes (pairing interactive .euclid-graphic SVG constructions with historical reception across antiquity, the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and modern geometry).

Books of the Treatise¶

Liber I — De Maximo Absoluto

Nicholas of Cusa’s investigations into the Absolute Maximum, polygon exhaustion, and the three-step method of mathematical transsumption.

Nicholas of Cusa

Liber II — De Universo et Cosmologia

Nicholas of Cusa’s revolutionary centerless cosmology: motion in the contracted universe, relativity of observers, and the moving Earth as a noble star.

Nicholas of Cusa
