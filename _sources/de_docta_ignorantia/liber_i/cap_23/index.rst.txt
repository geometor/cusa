Book I, Chapter 23 — Transsumption of the Infinite Sphere to the Actual Existence of God
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Original GEOMETOR Translation from the Latin · h I, nn. 70–73 · Paris 1514 fol. 9v–10r · Rotta 1913 pp. 50–52*

.. _cusa-en-di-i-23-s-1:

.. folio:: DI I.23 §1

It is fitting to add a few further speculations concerning the infinite sphere. In an infinite sphere we find three maximal lines—of length, breadth, and depth—concurring at the center. Yet the center of the maximal sphere equals the diameter and the circumference. Within the infinite sphere, therefore, the center equals those three lines; nay rather, the center is all of them, namely length, breadth, and depth. Hence the Maximum will most simply and infinitely be all length, breadth, and depth, which within it are one most simple, indivisible Maximum. As center, it precedes every breadth, length, and depth, and is likewise their end and their middle, because in an infinite sphere center, thickness, and circumference are identical. Just as the infinite sphere is wholly in act and most simple, so the Maximum is most simply and wholly in act; and just as the sphere is the act of line, triangle, and circle, so the Maximum is the act of all things. Every actual existence, accordingly, derives from it whatever actuality it possesses, and exists in act just insofar as it exists within that infinite act. Therefore the Maximum is the Form of forms and the Form of being, or Maximal Actual Beingness.

.. _cusa-en-di-i-23-s-2:

.. folio:: DI I.23 §2

Hence Parmenides, reflecting with utmost subtlety, declared that God is He for whom to be anything that is, is to be everything that is. Just as the sphere is the ultimate perfection of figures, than which none is greater, so the Maximum is the most perfect perfection of all things, to such a degree that within it everything imperfect is most perfect—just as the infinite line is a sphere, curvature is rectitude, composition is simplicity, diversity is identity, otherness is unity, and so for all the rest. How indeed could any trace of imperfection reside where imperfection is infinite perfection and possibility is infinite act?

.. _cusa-en-di-i-23-s-3:

.. folio:: DI I.23 §3

Seeing that the Maximum is like the maximal sphere, we now behold clearly how it is the sole, simplest, and most adequate measure of the entire universe and of all things existing within the universe, because within it the whole is no greater than the part, just as the infinite sphere is no greater than the infinite line. God, therefore, is the sole and simplest ratio of the whole universe; and just as the sphere arises after infinite revolutions, so God—like the maximal sphere—is the simplest measure of all revolutions. For all quickening, motion, and intelligence come from Him, in Him, and through Him, before whom a single revolution of the eighth sphere is no smaller than infinitely many revolutions, since He is the goal of all motions, in whom every motion comes to rest as in its end. For He is maximal rest, within which all motion is rest; and thus maximal rest is the measure of all motions, just as maximal rectitude is the measure of all circumferences and maximal presence—or eternity—is the measure of all times. In Him, therefore, all natural motions rest as in their goal, and every potency is perfected in Him as in infinite act; and because He is the beingness of all being and every motion tends toward being, the rest of motion is He Himself who is motion's goal, namely the form and act of being.

.. _cusa-en-di-i-23-s-4:

.. folio:: DI I.23 §4

Toward Him, accordingly, all beings strive. And because they are finite and cannot participate in that goal equally in comparison with one another, some participate in this goal through the mediation of others—just as the line is led into the sphere through the mediation of the triangle and the circle, the triangle through the mediation of the circle, and the circle into the sphere directly through itself.
