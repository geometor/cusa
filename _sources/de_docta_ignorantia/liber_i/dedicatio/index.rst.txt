Dedicatory Prologue to Cardinal Julian Cesarini
-----------------------------------------------

*Original GEOMETOR Translation from the Latin · h I, n. 1 · Paris 1514 fol. 1r · Rotta 1913 p. 1*

.. _cusa-en-di-ded-s-1:

.. folio:: DI Ded. §1

To his revered teacher, the father beloved of God and most reverend Lord Julian, most worthy Cardinal of the Holy Apostolic See.

.. _cusa-en-di-ded-s-2:

.. folio:: DI Ded. §2

Your supreme and long-tested intellect will rightly wonder what I intend when, venturing somewhat incautiously to lay bare my unpolished awkwardnesses, I choose you as judge—as though any leisure remained to you amid the gravest public affairs of your cardinalatial office at the Apostolic See, and as though, after mastering the highest knowledge of all Latin writers who have hitherto shone forth and now of the Greeks as well, you could be drawn by the novelty of a title toward this perhaps clumsy conception of mine, when you have long known full well what measure of mind I possess. Yet I hope that this very wonder—not because you suppose anything hitherto unknown is set down here, but rather out of curiosity at the boldness by which I have been led to treat of learned ignorance—will entice your mind, ever eager for knowledge, to look within. Natural philosophers report that a certain sharp pang at the mouth of the stomach precedes appetite, so that nature, striving to preserve itself, may be aroused and restored. In just this way I judge that wonder, on account of which philosophy begins, runs ahead of the desire to know, so that the intellect, whose very being is to understand, may be brought to perfection through the study of truth. Uncommon things, even when monstrous, are wont to stir us. Therefore, matchless teacher, deem in your kindness that something of worth lies hidden here, and receive from a German such manner of reasoning in divine matters as immense toil has rendered deeply welcome to me.
