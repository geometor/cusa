===================================================
The Three-Step Method of Mathematical Transsumption
===================================================

.. rubric:: De Docta Ignorantia · Liber I, Capitulum XII (1440)

.. epigraph::

   *Primo necesse est figuras mathematicas finitas considerare cum suis passionibus et rationibus, et ipsas rationes correspondenter ad infinitas tales figuras transferre, post haec tertio adhuc altius ipsas rationes infinitarum figurarum transumere ad infinitum simplex absolutissimum etiam ab omni figura.*

   First we must consider finite mathematical figures with their properties and ratios, next transfer those ratios correspondingly to infinite figures of the same kind, and third transsume the ratios of those infinite figures still higher unto the Simple Infinite wholly absolved from every figure. — **De Docta Ignorantia I.12 §1**

Book I, Chapter 12 — How Mathematical Signs Ought To Be Used for Our Purpose
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Original GEOMETOR Translation from the Latin · h I, nn. 33–34 · Paris 1514 fol. 5r · Rotta 1913 pp. 25–27*

.. _cusa-en-di-i-12-s-1:

.. folio:: DI I.12 §1

Yet since it is established from what has gone before that the unqualified Maximum can be none of the things known or conceived by us, when we propose to investigate it symbolically we must leap beyond simple likeness. For inasmuch as all mathematical objects are finite and cannot even be imagined otherwise, if we wish to use finite things as an example for ascending to the unqualified Maximum, first we must consider finite mathematical figures together with their properties and intelligible ratios; second, we must transfer those very ratios correspondingly to infinite figures of the same kind; and third, after this, we must transsume the ratios of those infinite figures still higher unto the Simple Infinite that is wholly absolved even from every figure. Then at last our ignorance will be taught incomprehensibly how we, who labor in an enigma, ought to think more rightly and more truly concerning the Most High.

.. _cusa-en-di-i-12-s-2:

.. folio:: DI I.12 §2

Acting in this way, therefore, and setting out under the guidance of Maximal Truth, we note that holy men of most exalted mind who applied themselves to figures spoke in varied ways: most devout Anselm compared Maximal Truth to infinite rectitude; following him, let us turn to the figure of rectitude, which I picture as a straight line. Other very skilled thinkers compared to the superblessed Trinity a triangle of three equal and right angles; and because such a triangle necessarily consists of infinite sides, as will be shown, it may be called an infinite triangle—and these too we shall follow. Others, striving to figure infinite unity, called God an infinite circle. Those, finally, who contemplated the most actual existence of God affirmed God to be as it were an infinite sphere. We, however, shall demonstrate that all of these simultaneously conceived rightly concerning the Maximum, and that the judgment of them all is one and the same.
