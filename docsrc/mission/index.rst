=======
Mission
=======

.. rubric:: Nicholas of Cusa as the Philosophical & Projective Hinge of the GEOMETOR Canon

Standing chronologically and conceptually at the watershed between classical-medieval mathematics and early modern projective, infinitesimal, and relativistic thought, :person:`Nicholas of Cusa <cusanus>` (*Nicolaus Cusanus*, 1401–1464) transformed geometry from a static science of finite magnitudes into a **dynamic art of infinite limits and transsumptive vision**.

Within the 27-repository **GEOMETOR** ecosystem, ``geometor/cusa`` bridges four foundational traditions:

1. **From Ancient Exhaustion to Intellectual Limit Vision**:
   Inheriting :person:`Plato's <plato>` Divided Line (``geometor/plato``), :person:`Aristotle's <aristotle>` theory of measure and continuum (``geometor/aristotle``), :person:`Euclid's <euclid>` *Elements* (``geometor/euclid`` & ``geometor/elements``), :person:`Archimedes' <archimedes>` polygon exhaustion, and :person:`Proclus's <proclus>` philosophy of mathematical imagination (``geometor/proclus``), Cusa distinguishes discursive :term:`ratio <ratio-et-intellectus>`—which remains bound by non-contradiction among finite magnitudes—from :term:`intellectus <ratio-et-intellectus>`, which beholds the asymptotic limit where curved and straight coincide.
2. **Projective Continuity & Conformal Circle Geometry**:
   Four centuries before Poncelet's Principle of Continuity (1822), Möbius circle inversion, and :person:`Felix Klein's <klein>` Erlangen synthesis, *De Docta Ignorantia* ``I.13–15`` demonstrated that on the completed geometric horizon the straight line *is* the maximal circle and the maximal degenerate triangle—a truth realized with 100% algebraic precision in ``geometor.cusa`` via conformal inversion and :person:`Wildberger's <wildberger>` Rational Trigonometry (``geometor/wildberger``).
3. **Constructive Circle Rectification & Fifth-Order Padé Approximation**:
   Across *De Geometricis Transmutationibus* (1445), *De Mathematicis Complementis* (1453), *De Mathematica Perfectione* (1458), and *Aurea Propositio in Mathematicis* (1459), Cusa pioneered algebraic and geometric conservation laws for rectifying circular arcs—culminating in the three-ray formula :math:`\theta \approx \frac{3\sin\theta}{2 + \cos\theta}` later rediscovered by :person:`Snellius <snellius>` (1621) and proved optimal by :person:`Huygens <snellius>` (1654).
4. **Centerless Cosmology & Perspective Relativity**:
   In *De Docta Ignorantia* ``II.11–12``, Cusa deduced from the impossibility of exact physical equidistance that the earth moves as a *stella nobilis* and that every cosmic observer perceives himself at an apparent center—inspiring :person:`Kepler <kepler>` (*divinus mihi Cusanus*, 1596) and modern relativistic cosmology.
