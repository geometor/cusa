### Author's Epistolary Colophon to Cardinal Julian (Kues, February 12, 1440)
# Witness: h I, nn. 263–264 · Paris 1514 fol. 25v · Rotta 1913 pp. 188–189

[DI Ep. §1] Receive now, revered father, the things that I long desired to attain by diverse pathways of learning yet could not reach before, until, while returning by sea from Greece, I was led—by a gift from on high, I believe, from the Father of lights, from whom comes every best gift—to embrace incomprehensible things incomprehensibly in learned ignorance, by transcending the corruptible truths knowable in a human manner. This learned ignorance I have now brought to completion, in Him who is Truth, within these little books, which from the self-same principle can be either condensed or expanded. In these deep matters the whole endeavor of our human mind ought to be to raise itself unto that simplicity where contradictories coincide: with this the conception of the first book labors. From that principle the second book draws forth a few insights concerning the universe, uncommon to many and above the ordinary road of philosophers. And now at last I have completed the third book concerning ever-blessed Jesus, advancing always from the same foundation; and the Lord Jesus has continually grown greater to me in intellect and affection through the increase of faith. For no one holding the faith of Christ can deny that along this path he is kindled higher in desire, so that after long meditations and ascents he sees most sweet Jesus alone as worthy of love and, joyfully forsaking all things, embraces Him as true life and everlasting joy. To one who thus enters into Jesus all things give way, and neither any writings whatsoever nor this world can cast up difficulty, because such a one is transformed into Jesus through the Spirit of Christ indwelling him, who is the goal of intellectual desires; whom do you, most devoted father, earnestly entreat with suppliant heart on behalf of me, a most wretched sinner, that together we may merit to enjoy Him eternally.

[DI Ep. §2] Completed at Kues, February 12, 1440.
