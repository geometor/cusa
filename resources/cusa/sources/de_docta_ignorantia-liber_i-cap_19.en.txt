### Book I, Chapter 19 — Transsumption of the Infinite Triangle to the Maximal Trinity
# Witness: h I, nn. 55–58 · Paris 1514 fol. 7v–8r · Rotta 1913 pp. 40–42

[DI I.19 §1] Let us now be instructed in ignorance from what has been stated and proved, namely that the maximal line is the maximal triangle. The maximal line has been shown to be a triangle; and because a line is most simple, the most simple will be triune. Every angle of that triangle will be the line, since the whole triangle is the line; therefore the infinite line is triune. Yet there cannot be several infinites; therefore that trinity is unity.

[DI I.19 §2] Furthermore, since in geometry it is demonstrated that the angle opposite the greater side is greater, and here we have a triangle possessing none save infinite sides, its angles will be maximal and infinite. Hence no one angle is smaller than the others, nor are two greater than the third; rather, just as outside infinite quantity no quantity can exist, so outside one infinite angle no other angles can exist. Consequently each will be in the other, and all three will be one Maximum. Moreover, just as the maximal line is no more line than triangle, circle, or sphere, but in truth is all of them without composition, as has been shown, so in like manner the unqualified Maximum as linear Maximum may be called Essence; as triangular, Trinity; as circular, Unity; and as spherical, Actual Existence.

[DI I.19 §3] The Maximum, therefore, is triune essence, one in act; nor is essence other than trinity, nor trinity other than unity, nor actuality other than unity, trinity, or essence, even though it is most true that the Maximum is all these identically and most simply. Just as it is true that the Maximum exists and is one, so it is true that it is triune in such a manner that the truth of trinity does not contradict most simple unity, but is that unity itself.

[DI I.19 §4] Now this is possible in no other way than as can be attained through the likeness of the maximal triangle. Knowing from what precedes that true triangle and most simple line, therefore, we shall attain the Trinity in learned ignorance in the manner possible to man. For we observe that we do not find first one angle, next another, and third yet another, as in finite triangles, since one and another and another cannot exist within the unity of the triangle without composition. Instead, the One exists triply without numerical multiplication; wherefore most learned Augustine rightly said: 'Once you begin to count the Trinity, you step outside the truth.' In divine matters one must embrace contradictories within a simple concept so far as possible, anticipating them antecedently: for instance, in divine things one must not conceive distinction and indistinction as two contradictories, but conceive them antecedently in their most simple principle, where distinction is no other than indistinction; then one conceives clearly that trinity and unity are the same. Where distinction is indistinction, trinity is unity; and conversely, where indistinction is distinction, unity is trinity. So too with plurality of persons and unity of essence: where plurality is unity, trinity of persons is identical with unity of essence; and conversely, where unity is plurality, unity of essence is trinity in the persons.

[DI I.19 §5] All this appears clearly in our geometric example, where the most simple line is a triangle and conversely the simple triangle is linear unity. Here too we see why the angles of the triangle cannot be numbered as one, two, three, since each is in each—as the Son declares: 'I am in the Father and the Father is in me.' Again, the truth of a triangle requires three angles: here, then, there are most truly three angles, each one maximal, and all of them one Maximum. The truth of a triangle requires further that one angle not be another; and here likewise the truth of the unity of most simple essence requires that those three angles not be three separate things, but one—and this too holds true here. Join together antecedently, therefore, these things that seem opposed, as I said before, and you will have not 'one and three' or the reverse, but the Unitrine or Triune. And this is absolute truth.
