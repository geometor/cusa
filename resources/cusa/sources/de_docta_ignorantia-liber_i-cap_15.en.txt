### Book I, Chapter 15 — That That Triangle Is a Circle and a Sphere
# Witness: h I, nn. 40–41 · Paris 1514 fol. 6r · Rotta 1913 pp. 31–32

[DI I.15 §1] Next it will be seen still more clearly that this triangle is a circle. Let the triangle abc be generated by supposition through swinging the line ab around while a remains fixed until b arrives at c. Beyond doubt, if the line ab were infinite and b were swung completely around until it returned to its starting-point, a maximal circle would be generated, of which bc is a portion. Now because bc is a portion of an infinite arc, bc is a straight line. And since every part of the infinite is infinite, bc is therefore not smaller than the whole arc of the infinite circumference. Hence bc will be not merely a portion, but the most complete circumference itself. Consequently the triangle abc must be the maximal circle.

[DI I.15 §2] Moreover, because the circumference bc is a straight line, it is not greater than the infinite line ab, since nothing is greater than the infinite; nor are there two lines, because there cannot be two infinites. Therefore the infinite line, which is a triangle, is also a circle. Which was proposed.

[DI I.15 §3] Furthermore, that the infinite line is a sphere becomes utterly manifest as follows: the line ab is the circumference of the maximal circle—nay rather, it is the circle itself, as has already been proved—and in the triangle it was carried from b to c, as stated above. Yet bc is the infinite line, as has likewise just been proved. Therefore ab has returned upon itself in c with a complete return upon itself. And when this happens, it follows of necessity that a sphere has arisen from such a revolution of a circle upon itself. Because it was proved above that abc is circle, triangle, and line, we have now proved that it is also a sphere. And these are the things we set out to investigate.
