### Book I, Chapter 13 — On the Properties of the Maximal and Infinite Line
# Witness: h I, nn. 35–36 · Paris 1514 fol. 5r–5v · Rotta 1913 pp. 27–29 (restoring Klibansky's collation on arc gh)

[DI I.13 §1] I declare, therefore, that if there were an infinite line, it would be a straight line, it would be a triangle, it would be a circle, and it would be a sphere; and in like manner, if there were an infinite sphere, it would be a circle, a triangle, and a line; and the very same must be said concerning an infinite triangle and an infinite circle.

[DI I.13 §2] First, that an infinite line is straight is plain: the diameter of a circle is a straight line, whereas the circumference is a curved line greater than the diameter. If, then, the curved line admits less curvature the larger the circle whose circumference it is, the circumference of the maximal circle—which cannot be greater—is minimally curved, and therefore maximally straight. Thus the Minimum coincides with the Maximum, so that before our very eyes it appears necessary that the maximal line be maximally straight and minimally curved. Nor can the slightest scruple of doubt linger here when in the figure alongside one sees how the arc cd of the larger circle recedes further from curvature than the arc ef of the smaller circle, and that arc recedes further from curvature than the arc gh of a still smaller circle; wherefore the straight line ab will be the arc of the maximal circle, which cannot be greater. Thus we see how a maximal and infinite line is necessarily most straight, to which curvature is not opposed—nay rather, within that maximal line itself curvature is rectitude; and this was the first point to be proved.

[DI I.13 §3] Second, it was stated that the infinite line is the maximal triangle, circle, and sphere. To demonstrate this, we must observe in finite lines what lies in the potency of a finite line; and because whatever lies in the potency of a finite line is what the infinite line is in act, what we seek will become clearer to us.

[DI I.13 §4] First we know that a finite line can be longer and straighter in length, and it has already been proved that the maximal line is longest and straightest. Second, if the line ab, while the point a remains fixed, is swung around until b arrives at c, a triangle arises; if the revolution is completed until b returns to the starting-point where it began, a circle is produced. Again, if while a remains fixed, b is swung around until it reaches the place opposite where it began—let it be d—from the lines ab and ad one continuous line is formed and a semicircle is described. And if, while the diameter bd remains fixed, the semicircle is revolved around it, a sphere arises; and this sphere itself is the ultimate realization of the potency of the line, existing wholly in act, because the sphere does not stand in potency toward any further figure. If, therefore, these figures reside in the potency of a finite line, and the infinite line is in act all things toward which a finite line is in potency, it follows that the infinite line is triangle, circle, and sphere. Which was to be proved.

[DI I.13 §5] And since perhaps you would wish to see still more clearly how the infinite is in act those things that lie in the potency of the finite, I shall render you most certain of this as well.
