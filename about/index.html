About¶

Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge & Repurposing It for Exact Symbolic Reasoning

About This Edition¶

geometor/cusa establishes the authoritative Digital Scriptorium critical edition and exact symbolic geometry suite for Nicholas of Cusa’s (Nicolaus Cusanus, 1401–1464) foundational philosophical and mathematical writings. It unites the mathematical core of *De Docta Ignorantia* (1440) with his four constructive mathematical treatises: *De Geometricis Transmutationibus* (1445), *De Mathematicis Complementis* (1453), *De Mathematica Perfectione* (1458), and the complete *Aurea Propositio in Mathematicis* (1459).

Standing at the watershed between classical-medieval mathematics and early modern projective, infinitesimal, and relativistic thought, Cusa transformed geometry from a static science of finite magnitudes into a dynamic art of infinite limits and transsumptive vision. In De Docta Ignorantia, Cusa distinguished discursive ratio—which remains bound by non-contradiction among finite magnitudes—from intellectus, which beholds the asymptotic limit where curved and straight coincide. Four centuries before Jean-Victor Poncelet’s Principle of Continuity, August Ferdinand Möbius’s circle inversion, and Felix Klein’s Erlangen Program, Cusa demonstrated that on the completed geometric horizon the straight line is the maximal circle and the maximal degenerate triangle. Across his subsequent mathematical opuscula, he developed geometric and algebraic conservation laws for rectifying circular arcs, anticipating the optimal three-ray approximations of Snellius and Huygens.

Within the GEOMETOR canon, geometor/cusa serves as the philosophical and projective hinge bridging classical Greek synthetic geometry (geometor/plato, geometor/aristotle, geometor/euclid, geometor/archimedes, geometor/proclus) and modern exact symbolic algebra (via SymPy and geometor.model), conformal circle inversion, and Norman Wildberger’s Rational Trigonometry (geometor/wildberger). The complete treatise is presented across a synchronized 4-track Digital Scriptorium Book Codex (English translation, collated Latin witnesses, executive summaries, and critical notes) paired with runnable symbolic demonstrations.

The GEOMETOR Mission¶

The GEOMETOR initiative, founded by phi ARCHITECT, is dedicated to a three-fold synthesis of classical geometry, exact symbolic computation, and modern knowledge architecture:

  • Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge: Moving beyond passive page scans and static OCR to recover the living deductive structure of 2,300+ years of geometric, axiomatic, and philosophical treatises. We elevate running prose, marginal citations, primary-source folios (via publish folio), and calibrated Ink-to-Vector .euclid-graphic diagrams into structured, machine-addressable semantic graphs where every enunciation, proof step, and historical cross-reference is individually referenceable.

  • Exact Symbolic Grounding (``geometor.model``): Anchoring geometric constructions in 100% exact symbolic algebra (via SymPy) and Rational Trigonometry invariants (quadrance, spread, cross-ratio) over exact algebraic number fields rather than lossy floating-point approximations. Every geometric theorem is treated as an exact algebraic truth verifiable through computational modeling.

  • Repurposing Knowledge Across Mediums: Re-deploying extracted semantic and algebraic structures across synchronized multi-track Book Codices and EPUB 3 editions (via photon-platform/publish), computable deductive canons (geometor.elements, geometor.etc, geometor.pappus, geometor.divine), interactive SVG laboratories (geometor.explorer), QHD visual broadcasts, and neuro-symbolic program synthesis (geometor.seer) across geometor.com.

Sources, Provenance & Methodology¶

All primary Latin texts in geometor/cusa derive from public-domain witnesses verified by multimodal archival scan inspection, including the Paris 1514 First Edition, the Basel 1565 Opera, and Paolo Rotta’s 1913 Laterza edition (with two typesetting homeoteleuton omissions restored via Raymond Klibansky’s annotated copy at McGill University Library).

Every English sentence across all five Codex units was freshly translated directly from the Latin witnesses specifically for GEOMETOR and is released under the permissive MIT License.
