De Mathematicis Complementis¶

Scripta Mathematica (1445–1459) — Nicholas of Cusa

Nicholas of Cusa (Nicolaus Cusanus, 1401–1464)
Cardinal Priest of San Pietro in Vincoli · Bishop of Brixen

First Edition: Paris (Jodocus Badius Ascensius, 1514, ed. Jacques Lefèvre d’Étaples)
Critical Editions: Heidelberg (Opera Omnia XX, 2010) · Paris / Basel (1514 / 1565)
GEOMETOR Digital Edition: 100% Original English Translation with Synchronized Latin Witness

De Mathematicis Complementis and Cusa’s companion Scripta Mathematica represent the cardinal’s constructive geometric treatises composed between 1445 and 1459. Spanning isoperimetric polygons, circle rectification, the critical dissection of Archimedes’ spiral, and the Golden Proposition (Aurea Propositio), these works apply the principles of learned ignorance to constructive geometry.

Treatises & Mathematical Writings¶

De Geometricis Transmutationibus — Isoperimetric Extrema and the Sesquiquartal Radius

Omnium figurarum isoperimetrarum capacior est circulus, et minimae capacitatis triangulus. Inter has extremitates omnes aliae figurae continentur.

Of all isoperimetric figures the circle has greatest capacity, and the triangle least capacity; between these extremes all other figures are contained. — De Geometricis Transmutationibus §1

De Mathematicis Complementis — Inradius, Circumradius, and Archimedes’ Spiral

Prima linea est perpendicularis a centro ad latus polygoniae ducta; secunda linea est a centro ad angulum producta. Crescente numero angulorum prima et secunda linea coincidunt in radio circuli.

The first line is the perpendicular drawn from center to polygon side; the second line is drawn from center to angle. As the number of angles increases, first and second line coincide in the radius of the circle. — De Mathematicis Complementis §3

De Mathematica Perfectione — Vanishing Sagitta and Intellectual Vision

Visio intellectualis transit supra omnem rationem discursivam, ubi minimum chordae coincidit cum minimo arcus.

Intellectual vision rises above all discursive reason to where the minimal chord coincides with the minimal arc. — De Mathematica Perfectione §2

Aurea Propositio in Mathematicis — Three-Ray Sum Invariance and the Golden Proposition

Ipsa est aurea propositio, in qua per rectam lineam rectificatur circuli circumferentia, manente summa trium radiorum aequali tribus semidiametris.

This is the Golden Proposition, wherein through a straight line the circle’s circumference is rectified while the sum of the three rays remains equal to three semidiameters. — Aurea Propositio in Mathematicis §1
